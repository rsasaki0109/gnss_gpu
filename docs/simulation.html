Data sources, method & limits

Building and bridge components, with original interior rings.

Base map: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Current roads, water and place labels provide context beneath the PLATEAU mesh. The raster map follows a nominal sea-level reference surface; it supplies no terrain or signal-obstruction geometry. Its date differs from the recorded drive and city model. Only tiles for the displayed camera view are requested, following the OSM tile usage policy. A map outage does not stop the signal simulation.

City: PLATEAU Tokyo 23 wards, 2022 (CC BY 4.0), Odaiba building and bridge CityGML. The highest available LoD is selected per component. T.P. heights are converted to ellipsoidal heights with bilinear GSI GSIGEO2011 ver.2.1 corrections at every vertex. The geoid grid is used offline and is not redistributed.

Trajectory & sky: UrbanNav Tokyo Odaiba, 2018-12-19, Applanix POS LV620 reference ECEF coordinates and DLR/TUM CONGO/MGEX broadcast navigation data. GPS positions use healthy, already-transmitted ephemerides within their fit interval, Kepler propagation, harmonic corrections, iterative signal travel time and Earth rotation. Times are GPST. UrbanNav MIT attribution ↗.

Tracing: All triangles participate in Float64 BVH visibility tests. Single-bounce specular candidates are tested against each triangle, then both legs are checked for obstruction. Reflections use quasi-static satellite geometry per epoch. ¹ No path means no unobstructed direct or single-bounce ray inside the loaded mesh. Display rays are shortened; display buffers alone use Float32.

Source topology repair is recorded in the scene manifest.

² Controlled range experiment: Horizontal displacement from iterative least squares using the earliest modeled path per usable satellite. It starts at the reference position, with ideal clock and atmospheric corrections, zero noise and no power threshold. LOS-only displacement is zero by construction when geometry permits a solution. This is geometric bias, not measured positioning accuracy, a receiver simulator, or a native PF/FGO benchmark.

Reconstruction limits: A 2018 drive is combined with a 2022 city model, so this does not reproduce the exact historical scene. JGD2011 horizontal coordinates are nominally aligned with WGS84; no epoch-dependent crustal correction or reference-to-antenna lever-arm calibration is applied. This is not survey-grade frame registration. Moving objects, vegetation, RF power/materials, diffraction, multiple bounces, atmosphere and receiver tracking are not modeled. Occlusion is limited to the 28 loaded tiles. Playback shows the latest completed epoch; it does not compute every intermediate epoch in real time. The timeline evaluates any recorded pose exactly.

Route navigation: The north-up route overview retains every recorded position. Click or tap near its line to select the closest recorded pose on screen; exact spatial ties prefer the current time, then the earlier source row. The highlighted segment spans up to five seconds on either side. Selection pauses playback. An amber marker indicates that the selected pose is still being traced; a green marker confirms that the displayed trace matches the selected pose and receiver controls. The route overview supports 0.1-second arrow steps, one-second Shift + arrow steps and Home / End. In the 3D view, arrow keys orbit and Space plays or pauses.

Surface inspection: Show reflector frames the receiver and the shortest valid single-bounce reflection surface; Show obstruction frames the first triangle blocking the direct ray. These views isolate the chosen path and tint its original triangle while retaining the full city and normal depth occlusion. The camera faces the receiver side of the surface; a view from below is labeled Underside view. The caption identifies the satellite and interaction, and the CityGML feature below the inspector follows the focused surface. Follow receiver restores the preceding camera view. Changing satellite, epoch or receiver settings, resuming playback, or hiding a focused reflection leaves surface inspection. Camera and display controls use the completed trace without changing or rerunning its calculations.

Scene links: Share this scene pauses playback and waits for the selected pose to finish tracing. Links preserve its original epoch, virtual height offset, elevation mask, selected satellite, view mode, camera orientation/zoom and display toggles. Values are serialized at full JavaScript Number precision. The link checks the mesh and scene asset checksums before restoring inputs; incompatible data or invalid settings show a visible message and the default scene. An unavailable satellite or surface is reported explicitly. Results are recomputed with the currently published numerical model. The link state stays in the URL fragment. Clipboard restrictions leave a selectable link for manual copying.

Receiver comparison: Pin a completed result, then vary the virtual height or elevation mask. The comparison uses completed traces at the same original epoch and GPS time; a different pose or pending trace hides all differences. Return to pinned pose preserves the current receiver controls. The tables show current minus pinned counts and ideal-range horizontal geometric bias, plus each satellite's path class and earliest excess range. LOS excess is zero even when additional reflections exist; blocked and masked paths have no usable excess. Unavailable position solutions remain unavailable and have no numeric difference. Highlighted rows indicate a path-class or mask change, not a measured accuracy improvement. Pinning, clearing and viewing the comparison do not launch new traces. The pinned result stays in the current tab until cleared or reloaded; its scene link preserves the inputs and view captured at pinning.

Sky plot: Circles reuse the completed trace's geometric satellite azimuths and elevations, with geodetic north up and east right. Distance from the center is proportional to zenith angle; the center is 90° elevation and the outer circle is the 0° horizon. The shaded rim is the completed satellite elevation mask, not a building skyline. Only GPS satellites above that mask are shown. Colors use the same direct, reflected-only or blocked classification as the full-mesh trace. Marker positions stay exact while labels can move to reduce overlap. The selected signal's diamond points from the receiver toward its existing shortest single-bounce reflection point, transformed from scene coordinates into the receiver's own geodetic ENU frame. This source bearing is opposite to the final ray's propagation velocity. Its angular separation from the satellite is calculated in 3D; the dashed line only connects their plotted directions. For LOS, the direct arrival coincides with the satellite and arrives before any displayed reflection. For NLOS, the shortest reflection is the earliest modeled path. Blocked signals have no modeled arrival. Reflected directions below the satellite mask are retained; those below the local horizon keep their signed elevation in the readout and are not plotted. These directions do not predict received power or receiver tracking. Point selection pauses playback and selects the same signal in the list and 3D view, without launching a trace. Pending results retain the previous completed satellite plot with its own time/mask label and disable point selection; reflected arrival overlays and values wait for the selected trace. Pause to inspect waits for the selected pose to finish.
